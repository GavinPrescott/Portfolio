    <div class="statement">
      <p>I feel as though people underestimate the amount of technology that surrounds them in their daily life.</p>

      <p>Think about how you wake up each morning, how you make your meals, and how you travel. Alarm clocks, toasters, cars, and cell phones may come to mind at first, but what about ones you don't notice?</p>

      <p>Paved roads, comfortable beds, and the machine that presses your morning orange juice. They are some of an unimaginably large list of technologies that you may not think of at first, but were once something people lived without. At one point someone dealt with a problem; a bumpy road broke an axle, waking up sore and unrested, or even just being tired of hand pressing oranges. When humans run into problems, they come up with solutions.</p>

      <p>My background in Anthropology really brought this idea to light, learning how ordinary people lived throughout history and how they interacted and shaped the world around them. <strong>Problems require solutions, but solutions require people to create them.</strong></p>

      <p>That's what pulled me into Engineering, and Robotics is where I landed. Robotics handles a large variety of different subjects and focuses on how they all come together as one cohesive piece. Mechanics, electronics, control, and software all converge onto something that acts in the physical world. What interests me is robots that do what humans can't, not what humans can already do. I aspire to be someone who can come up with solutions and solve problems that change lives. I want to create robots that can extend what we can reach, sense, and build.</p>
    </div>
